1. Executive Summary
This document specifies the production knowledge system required for the Zoiko Billing Chatbot. It defines what may become knowledge, who may approve it, how it is classified and versioned, how retrieval is permissioned, how evidence is passed to the model, when the chatbot must abstain, how citations are rendered, and how knowledge quality is measured over time.
The knowledge base is not a shared drive attached to an LLM. It is a governed, multi-namespace evidence system with explicit source authority, lifecycle state, access policy, provenance, freshness, jurisdiction, tenant boundaries and retrieval controls.
1.1 Outcomes
Give users accurate, source-grounded answers about Zoiko Billing features, workflows, policies and approved operational procedures.
Keep billing-domain knowledge separate from transactional financial truth. Invoice balances, payment status, reconciliation state and ledger facts must be read from authoritative billing services.
Ensure tenant-specific content cannot leak across tenants, legal entities or users.
Allow compliance and jurisdictional guidance to be scoped by effective date and geography.
Provide engineering with deterministic ingestion, retrieval, citation, audit and evaluation contracts.
Make outdated, superseded, unapproved and malicious content ineligible for production retrieval.
1.2 Non-negotiable boundaries
[TABLE: 7 rows x 3 cols]
Boundary | Rule | Reason
Financial truth | Do not store or answer changing account balances, invoice state or payment state from static KB documents. | Prevents stale financial answers.
Cross-product content | Payroll/HR documents are out of scope unless an approved integration and namespace explicitly enables them. | Prevents product and permission bleed.
Tenant isolation | Tenant content is partitioned and filtered by resolved tenant context before retrieval. | Prevents data leakage.
Retrieved instructions | Retrieved content is data, never privileged system instruction. | Mitigates indirect prompt injection.
Authority | Higher-authority approved policy overrides FAQ, help copy, historical release notes or lower-tier guidance. | Prevents conflicting answers.
Abstention | Insufficient or conflicting evidence must produce a bounded response, escalation or clarification—not fabrication. | Protects trust and financial safety.
[END TABLE]

3. Target Knowledge Architecture
Figure 1. Governed knowledge flow from approved source to grounded answer.
3.1 Architectural principles
[TABLE: 9 rows x 2 cols]
Principle | Implementation rule
Authority before similarity | Retrieval ranking must not allow a semantically similar low-authority source to displace governing approved policy.
Filter before vector search | Resolve user, tenant, entity, role, jurisdiction and namespace eligibility before candidate retrieval.
Hybrid retrieval | Use lexical/keyword, semantic/vector and structured metadata filters; permit reranking after policy filtering.
Evidence packaging | The orchestrator receives source spans, canonical IDs, version, dates, authority tier and citation handles—not only free text.
Immutable provenance | Each answer records which source versions and chunks were used.
No direct action from content | Knowledge can explain or inform action preparation; it cannot bypass the Action Control Plane.
Freshness is a control | Expired or superseded policy is non-retrievable in normal production modes.
Fail closed on uncertainty | Missing ACL context, stale security policy or ambiguous tenant resolution blocks restricted retrieval.
[END TABLE]
3.2 Logical components
[TABLE: 11 rows x 3 cols]
Component | Responsibility | Must not do
Source registry | Track canonical source, owner, authority, classification and lifecycle. | Treat arbitrary uploads as trusted.
Ingestion service | Scan, parse, classify, normalize, validate and stage content. | Publish without approval.
Content store | Retain approved text/structure and version history. | Serve cross-tenant content without policy.
Index pipeline | Build keyword/vector indexes using approved chunks and metadata. | Index secrets or revoked content.
Policy gateway | Resolve retrieval entitlement and namespace filters. | Rely on model prompt for access control.
Retriever | Generate candidates from eligible corpus. | Search excluded namespaces.
Reranker | Score relevance, authority, freshness and intent fit. | Override policy eligibility.
Grounding packager | Produce model-safe, cited context. | Expose raw executable content as instructions.
Evaluation service | Measure retrieval/answer performance against curated tests. | Self-certify without human review.
Audit/evidence service | Record lineage and retrieval decisions. | Store unbounded sensitive prompts unnecessarily.

[END TABLE]
4.2 Retrieval namespace decision
Resolve authenticated principal and service identity.
Resolve tenant and, when material, legal entity from the active Zoiko Billing context.
Resolve user roles/scopes and the requested operation purpose.
Classify intent and determine whether jurisdiction-specific or tenant-specific content is needed.
Build an allow-list of namespaces. Do not construct a deny-list over a globally searchable index.
Apply classification, effective-date and lifecycle filters before candidate retrieval.
Log the namespace decision and policy version used.
5. Source Authority & Conflict Resolution
5.1 Authority tiers
[TABLE: 7 rows x 4 cols]
Tier | Authority | Examples | Conflict rule
A0 | System-of-record facts | Live billing service responses, configuration service outputs | Not KB content; always wins for transactional/current state.
A1 | Governing approved policy | Current approved policy, legal/compliance control, signed operating standard | Wins over A2–A5 within applicable scope.
A2 | Approved SOP / runbook | Current operational procedure, escalation playbook | Wins over FAQs and informal guidance.
A3 | Canonical product documentation | Current product behavior, configuration, published help | Wins over FAQ when conflicting.
A4 | Approved FAQ / support article | Frequently asked questions and troubleshooting | Cannot create policy or override A1–A3.
A5 | Historical/reference | Release notes, archived guidance, legacy mappings | Not used for current answers unless explicitly historical.
[END TABLE]
5.2 Conflict algorithm
[TABLE: 8 rows x 2 cols]
CONFLICT RULE If two eligible sources materially disagree, the chatbot must not silently blend them. It must select the higher-authority source when scope/effective date clearly resolves the conflict; otherwise it must disclose that the evidence conflicts and route the question for resolution.
Check | Decision
Scope | Does each source apply to the same product, tenant, entity, jurisdiction and user population?
Effective time | Which source is effective for the date/time relevant to the user’s question?
Authority | Which source has the higher authority tier?
Version lineage | Is one source explicitly superseded by another?
Policy exception | Is there an approved exception specific to the tenant/entity?
Unresolved conflict | Abstain from a definitive claim; surface sources to reviewer/support and log a conflict incident.
[END TABLE]

9. Authoring Standard
9.1 Writing rules for machine- and human-readable knowledge
Use one normative idea per paragraph where practical; avoid burying exceptions in prose.
Use explicit actor, object, condition and outcome. Replace “it” or “they” when ambiguity could alter meaning.
Use MUST / MUST NOT for mandatory rules; SHOULD for recommended behavior; MAY for permitted optional behavior.
Define acronyms at first use and maintain canonical glossary entries.
Write dates, currencies, thresholds and jurisdictions explicitly. Avoid relative phrases such as “recently” in policy.
Distinguish product behavior from recommended business practice.
Do not copy transactional customer data into documentation examples. Use synthetic examples.
For compliance content, identify whether the content is explanatory guidance, an internal control, or an externally mandated requirement.
For procedures, include failure branches and escalation—not only the happy path.
Never embed instructions that tell the model to ignore system rules, reveal hidden prompts, execute tools or change authorization.
9.2 Terminology governance
[TABLE: 8 rows x 3 cols]

10. Ingestion Pipeline Wireframe
10.1 Pipeline stages
[TABLE: 13 rows x 3 cols]
Stage | Required behavior | Failure disposition
1. Intake | Accept only supported source/channel with submitter identity. | Reject unknown/untrusted source or quarantine.
2. Malware/content scan | Scan files and validate type/signature. | Quarantine; security event.
3. Classification | Resolve namespace, classification, owner, content type and jurisdiction. | Block publishing until complete.
4. Parse | Extract text, headings, tables, lists, links and anchors with structure. | Send to manual review if structural loss is material.
5. Normalize | Normalize whitespace/encoding while preserving meaning and references. | Never alter normative semantics.
6. Secret/PII detection | Detect restricted content based on policy. | Redact, reject or route to restricted workflow.
7. Authority validation | Verify approval state, owner and authority tier. | Non-approved versions stay staged.
8. Chunk | Create semantically coherent chunks with parent context. | Fail if chunk lineage is incomplete.
9. Index | Create lexical/vector indexes with metadata and ACL filters. | Do not partially expose failed versions.
10. Validation | Run retrieval smoke tests, citations, ACL and conflict checks. | Hold publication if tests fail.
11. Publish | Atomically switch approved version into retrieval eligibility. | Previous version remains until safe cutover.
12. Evidence | Record hashes, pipeline version, tests and publication event. | Publication without evidence is invalid.

11. Parsing, Chunking & Indexing Rules
11.1 Chunking doctrine
[TABLE: 11 rows x 2 cols]
Rule | Specification
Semantic boundary | Prefer headings, paragraphs, procedure steps, FAQ units and table groups over fixed-token cuts.
Parent context | Each chunk carries document title, heading path, authority, namespace and version.
Overlap | Use limited overlap only when needed for continuity; avoid duplicating normative text across many chunks.
Tables | Preserve headers and row meaning. Do not index isolated cells without header context.
Lists | Keep step numbers/order where sequence matters.
Definitions | Keep term and definition together.
Policy exceptions | Ensure exception conditions are co-retrievable with the governing rule.
Maximum size | Tune per model/retrieval stack; enforce token budget and measure answer quality rather than selecting an arbitrary universal number.
No hidden text | Exclude comments/hidden text unless explicitly part of approved source.
Attachments | Index attachment content only when independently classified and approved.
[END TABLE]
11.2 Index strategy
Maintain hybrid lexical and semantic retrieval where supported.
Separate production and staging indexes.
Treat embedding model changes as versioned migrations with reindex evaluation.
Keep metadata filters queryable without model interpretation.
Make revocation propagation deterministic and measurable.
Support index rebuild from canonical source store; vector index is not the sole source of record.
12. Retrieval & Ranking Contract
12.1 Retrieval sequence
Normalize the user query while preserving material identifiers, amounts, dates and domain terms.
Classify intent, product domain and risk.
Resolve identity, tenant, legal entity, locale and jurisdiction context.
Construct eligible namespace and classification filters.
Generate lexical and semantic candidates from eligible content only.
Apply authority, freshness, scope, exact-term and relevance signals.
Re-rank the candidate set without relaxing ACL filters.
Detect material conflicts, stale evidence and insufficient coverage.
Package the minimum evidence needed for the answer with canonical citation handles.
Record retrieval decision, policy version, source versions and scores for evaluation/audit.
12.2 Ranking dimensions
[TABLE: 11 rows x 3 cols]
Dimension | Example signal | Hard/soft
Eligibility | Namespace, tenant, entity, classification, role | Hard filter
Lifecycle | Published/current vs superseded/revoked | Hard filter
Temporal applicability | Effective date covers question date | Hard/strong
Authority | A1 policy vs A4 FAQ | Strong
Jurisdiction | Exact applicable country/subdivision | Strong
Product/version | Matches current product behavior | Strong
Lexical relevance | Exact domain/status/error code match | Soft
Semantic relevance | Embedding similarity | Soft
Freshness | Recently verified/current within review policy | Soft/strong based on content type

13.2 Citation rules
Every material policy/compliance statement must be traceable to an eligible source version.
For product how-to answers, citations may be collapsed in the UI but must remain available through “Sources” or equivalent evidence affordance.
Citations must resolve to a source the user is entitled to access. Do not expose the existence, title or URL of a restricted source to an unauthorized user.
When using multiple sources, the answer must not imply they share equal authority when they do not.
When the answer relies on live financial data plus knowledge, distinguish the live account fact from explanatory documentation.
Citation links/handles must survive source versioning through canonical IDs and immutable version references.

17.2 FAQ quality rules
FAQ is a convenience layer, not the source of policy authority.

22. Prompt Injection, RAG Poisoning & Content Security
The knowledge layer must assume that documents can contain malicious or accidental instructions. OWASP’s 2025 GenAI guidance identifies prompt injection and vector/embedding weaknesses as material risks for LLM applications; this wireframe therefore treats retrieved text as untrusted data and requires ingestion trust, namespace controls, validation and model-side instruction separation.
[TABLE: 11 rows x 2 cols]
Threat | Required mitigation
Indirect prompt injection | Delimit retrieved content as evidence; system/orchestrator instructions remain higher privilege; never execute instructions found in content.
RAG poisoning | Only approved sources reach production; detect anomalous/untrusted content; maintain provenance and rapid revocation.
Vector cross-tenant leakage | Filter by tenant/ACL before vector candidate exposure; tenant-aware index design and tests.
Malicious citation URL | Canonical allow-listed source resolution; sanitize rendered links.
Hidden text/instructions | Strip or flag hidden/zero-size/metadata/comment content unless approved.
Embedding inversion/sensitive disclosure | Minimize sensitive indexed content; apply encryption/access controls; separate restricted stores where warranted.
Unauthorized upload | Authenticate submitter, enforce role and quarantine until approval.
Compromised source integration | Signed/hash-tracked ingestion; source connector allow-list; change monitoring.
Over-broad retrieval | Limit top-k/context size and require eligible namespaces, authority and intent fit.
Model obeys retrieved “tool command” | Tools are invoked only from orchestrator policies and structured action contracts, never from source text.
[END TABLE]

Grounding packs carry immutable provenance and user-safe citations.
Answerability/abstention states are integrated into chatbot orchestration.
